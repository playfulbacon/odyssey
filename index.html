HOW TO PLAY

Lay the phone flat between you, held vertically. One player sits at each end. You each own the half of the screen nearest you.

THE LINE

A paddle sits at each end of the screen. A line is stretched between them and the ball runs back and forth along it. Slide your finger anywhere on your half to move your paddle — the line swings with it. Steer the line so the ball crosses what you want to hit.

BOOST

Lift your finger to shove the ball away from you. For a moment it flies and turns orange. A lift does nothing to a ball coming the other way, and if you both lift at once the two shoves cancel and the ball keeps its normal pace.

While your finger is off the screen your paddle is frozen where you left it. Nothing else about who is holding matters anywhere in the game: when it is your turn to shove, your lift shoves, whatever the other player is doing.

READING THE FIELD

There are two things worth having and they are not the same thing. Gold buys upgrades, and that is all gold does. Points clear stages, and that is all points do — they are measured against the run target, and once the target is met they stop mattering. They are never spent, never carried forward and never totalled.

Both come from the same place: killing enemies. Nothing else on the field pays anything.

GOLD

Gold is never lying around waiting for you. Nothing spawns it. It exists only as loot: a dying enemy throws out a scatter of GOLD MOTES where it stood, and they fade if nobody comes and gets them.

So a kill pays twice, on two different clocks. The points land the instant it dies and can never be taken away. The gold is still on the floor, and you have to steer the line back across it before it goes. Bigger enemies drop more.

STRENGTH

Your ball has a strength, and so does every enemy — both numbers are written on them. A contact between the two is settled by one comparison:

  • Ball equal or greater — the enemy dies and pays points.
  • Ball weaker — the ball takes the damage instead, and is thrown back the way it came.

A shove adds 1 to the ball's strength for as long as it lasts, which is why the number on the ball goes up the moment it turns orange — a resting ball is 1, a boosted one is 2. BOOST STRENGTH raises what a shove adds, and it is the one upgrade you cannot do without: everything on the field gains strength as the runs go by.

Both halves of that lesson are on the field from run 1. A DRONE is strength 1 and dies to a resting ball; a BRUTE is strength 2 and will not die unless the ball arrives orange.

SHIELDS AND WEAK POINTS

Some enemies wear a steel ring with one or two orange weak points cut into it. The plating is inert — bounce off it all day and nothing happens to either of you. The weak point is the only way in, and it takes a boosted ball.

A weak point faces one end of the phone, and the ball can only enter through it while running away from that end. So on a CYCLOPS — one shield, one weak point — only one of you can be the one to shove, and it is whoever it is facing. The other player steers and keeps still. A JANUS has a weak point facing each of you, so either can take the shot; it is stronger to make up for it.

The ball reverses when it hits a shield, weak point or plating alike. Arriving at the right hole from the wrong side is just a wall.

ENEMIES

  • DRONE — strength 1, no shield. A resting ball matches it, so it dies to a plain pass.
  • BRUTE — strength 2, no shield. One point too strong for a resting ball: no trick to it, it just has to be shoved.
  • CYCLOPS — strength 2, one weak point. One player's job.
  • JANUS — strength 3, a weak point facing each of you.

Every run adds strength to everything on the field, so a piece that died to a plain pass last run may need a shove behind it this one. Read the numbers.

OBSTACLES

Red, worth nothing, and no strength to beat. Touching one costs health and throws the ball back, every time.

  • SLAB — a long bar, exactly where it landed.
  • SHARD — a slab the size of a chip, loose on the field and drifting.
  • ROTOR — a hub with two sweeping arms, every bit as lethal as the hub.

HEALTH AND LIVES

These are two separate things. Health is the ball's: it comes off one point at a time, whenever the ball hits an obstacle or loses a fight. Lives are how many balls you have left. When health runs out you lose a life, everything stops, and you both hold again to launch a fresh ball with full health.

LAUNCHING

At the start of a run, and after every lost life, both players hold their half until the bar fills.

RUNS

Every run gives you a clock and a number of points to reach — and points come only from kills, so a run is won by fighting, not by digging. Clear it and spend your gold on upgrades: boost strength, ball health, extra lives, extra time. Clearing with time to spare pays a gold bonus, because bonus points would buy nothing. Each run adds strength to everything on the field. Miss the target, or burn every life, and the game ends.

DESKTOP KEYS

Bottom player — A D slide, S boosts.
Top player — J L slide, K boosts.
A finger does two jobs at once; on a keyboard they are two keys. Steering keeps your side down, and stopping is not a shove. The boost is a tap of S or K: hold it to keep your side down without moving, and let go to shove.
The mouse also works as one player, and does both at once like a finger.

PLAYGROUND

No clock. No target. Endless lives. Pick a piece and learn it.
