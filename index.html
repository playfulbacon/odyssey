HOW TO PLAY

Lay the phone flat between you, held vertically. One player sits at each end. You each own the half of the screen nearest you.

THE LINE

A paddle sits at each end of the screen. A line is stretched between them and the ball runs back and forth along it. Slide your finger anywhere on your half to move your paddle — the line swings with it. Steer the line so the ball crosses what you want to hit.

BOOST

Lift your finger to shove the ball away from you. For a moment it flies and turns orange. A lift does nothing to a ball coming the other way, and if you both lift at once the two shoves cancel and the ball keeps its normal pace.

While your finger is off the screen your paddle is frozen where you left it. Nothing else about who is holding matters anywhere in the game: when it is your turn to shove, your lift shoves, whatever the other player is doing.

READING THE FIELD

Cool is points, red is a life. Shape says it a second time — collectables are an O, obstacles an X — so you never have to pick one signal out at speed.

O
Collectable. Cool. Mint sits still, blue moves. Never dangerous.
X
Obstacle. Red, every one of them. There is no way through.
■
ORANGE — a shield, the boosted ball that strips it, and the paddle whose shove is up next. One idea, one colour.

THE HOT END

The paddle the ball last came off turns orange, and the orange bleeds a little way down the rail before fading back into ink. Only one end is ever hot, and it swaps the moment the ball reaches the other paddle.

It is the same orange doing the same job: the hot paddle is the one whose lift boosts right now. The ball is running away from that player, so theirs is the shove that lands — and the orange trailing off down the rail points the way it would go. Whoever is lit is on.

Spend it and the paddle goes dark. The moment you lift, the colour leaves the paddle and moves onto the flare and the ball, coming back when the shove runs out. Orange on a paddle always means the same thing: your shove is here and you have not used it yet.

Beyond that the rail says nothing. It has no halves and no opinion about your fingers.

THE BALL IS THE READOUT

Colour alone says what the ball is doing. No rings, no outlines — just the fill. There are two states, and one of them is nothing:

  • Orange — boosting. It is flying, trailing orange behind it, and it strips a shield.
  • Ink — not boosting. It strips nothing.

Two things decide it: is a shove running, and is it pushing the way the ball is already going. Nothing else comes into it.

COLLECTABLES

All of the points are here. Two of them are free: the MOTE sits still, the DRIFTER is the same thing wandering and pays more for the trouble. Touch either and it is banked.

The other three are the income, and they are the only reason to boost. Same cool O underneath, wearing orange rings:

  • WARD — one ring. A single boosted pass takes it and banks the piece in the same touch.
  • SHELL — two rings.
  • VAULT — three rings, and the biggest payout on the field.

A plain ball passes straight through a ring and does nothing at all — it cannot hurt you, it just does not count. Each boosted pass strips ball power rings, and the pass that takes the last one banks it.

Because a ring needs a boost, the alternating lift is the basic rhythm of scoring: you each shove as the ball runs away from you, so it is orange in both directions.

OBSTACLES

Every obstacle is the same proposition: red, worth nothing, and a life on any contact. There is nothing to learn about one beyond where it is and where it is going.

  • SLAB — a long bar, exactly where it landed.
  • SHARD — a slab the size of a chip, loose on the field and drifting.
  • ROTOR — a hub with two sweeping arms, every bit as lethal as the hub.

The risk is not in touching them — it is that parking a line over a three-ring vault takes time, and something red is always on its way.

LAUNCHING

At the start of a run, and after every lost life, both players hold their half until the bar fills.

RUNS

Every run gives you a clock and a score to beat. Clear it and spend your points on upgrades — lives, ball power, run time, boost duration — before the next one. Each run the obstacles get meaner. Miss the target, or burn every life, and the game ends.

DESKTOP KEYS

Bottom player — A D slide, S boosts.
Top player — J L slide, K boosts.
A finger does two jobs at once; on a keyboard they are two keys. Steering keeps your side down, and stopping is not a shove. The boost is a tap of S or K: hold it to keep your side down without moving, and let go to shove.
The mouse also works as one player, and does both at once like a finger.

PLAYGROUND

No clock. No target. Endless lives. Pick a piece and learn it.
